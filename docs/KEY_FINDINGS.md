# Key Findings

## Overall Care-Seeking Timeliness

The dashboard represents **422 respondents** in the overall view.

- **Within 24 hours:** 36.5% (154 respondents)
- **After 24 hours:** 63.5% (268 respondents)

Delayed care-seeking is therefore more common than care-seeking within 24 hours in the displayed sample.

## Healthcare Facilities and First Actions

### Facility type
- Private clinic: **85.06%**
- Public hospital: **9.74%**
- Health center: **2.60%**
- Medical center: **2.60%**

### First action
- Health facility: **36.97%**
- Pharmacy/drug shop: **32.70%**
- Home remedy: **26.78%**
- Traditional healer: **3.55%**

## Referral and Hospitalization

- Referral given: **59%**
- No referral: **41%**
- Hospitalized: **341 (80.81%)**
- Not hospitalized: **81 (19.19%)**

## Education, Facility Type, and First Action

The Care-Seeking Pathways page displays differences in timely/delayed care-seeking across education categories, facility types, and first actions. These are descriptive group differences and should not be interpreted as causal effects.

## Knowledge & Perceptions

The dashboard examines danger-sign knowledge, timing knowledge, perceived seriousness of fever, and fever-definition knowledge in relation to care-seeking timeliness.

Some displayed percentages exceed 100% and require DAX/denominator validation before being used as findings.

## Access & Barriers

The dashboard examines healthcare accessibility, affordability, healthcare autonomy, and transport mode by care-seeking time. Some percentage measures on this page also require validation because values above 100% are not valid proportions.

## Interpretation

The dashboard provides a descriptive picture of child fever care-seeking behavior in the represented dataset. Formal statistical analysis would be needed to assess statistical association or causality.
