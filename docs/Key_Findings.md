# Key Findings

## Project
**Child Fever & Care-Seeking Power BI Dashboard**

## 1. Overall Care-Seeking Timeliness

The dataset contains **422 respondents**.

| Care-Seeking Category | Respondents | Percentage |
|---|---:|---:|
| After 24 Hours | 268 | 63.5% |
| Within 24 Hours | 154 | 36.5% |
| **Total** | **422** | **100%** |

Delayed care-seeking was more common than care-seeking within 24 hours in the dataset.

## 2. Education and Care-Seeking

Education level showed differences in care-seeking timing.

Respondents with **college-level education and above** had a higher proportion of timely care-seeking, with **31.1%** seeking care within 24 hours.

Lower education groups showed a greater proportion of delayed care-seeking.

## 3. Healthcare Facility and Care-Seeking

Delayed care-seeking predominated among respondents associated with several public healthcare facility categories.

The private clinic group showed a more balanced pattern:

- **44.5%** delayed care
- **55.5%** sought care within 24 hours

This describes the distribution observed in the dataset and does not establish that facility type caused differences in care-seeking timing.

## 4. First Action Taken

| First Action | Percentage |
|---|---:|
| Health facility | 36.97% |
| Pharmacy/Drug shop | 32.70% |
| Home remedies | 26.78% |
| Traditional healer | 3.55% |

Traditional healer and home-remedy first actions were predominantly associated with delayed care-seeking, while initial health-facility contact showed a higher proportion of timely care-seeking.

## 5. Healthcare Access

Timely care-seeking was more commonly observed among respondents reporting better physical accessibility and more affordable healthcare services.

These findings describe patterns within the dataset and should not be interpreted as causal effects.

## 6. Household Decision-Making

Joint decision-making by both spouses showed different distributions between care-seeking groups:

- **41.47%** among respondents seeking care after 24 hours
- **29.62%** among respondents seeking care within 24 hours

This indicates a descriptive difference in the distribution of household decision-making across care-seeking groups.

## 7. Knowledge, Perception and Danger Signs

Knowledge and perceptions related to childhood fever showed notable differences between timely and delayed care-seeking groups.

Respondents who lacked knowledge of fever danger signs were more represented among those seeking care after 24 hours:

- **50.95%** after 24 hours
- **4.74%** within 24 hours

Recognizing fever as serious, understanding appropriate timing for action, and having a correct conceptual understanding of fever also showed differences across care-seeking groups.

## 8. Healthcare Pathway and Outcomes

### Referral

- Formal referral: **41.00%**
- No formal referral: **59.00%**

### Hospitalization

- Hospitalized: **19.19% (81)**
- Not hospitalized: **80.81% (341)**

### Clinical Outcome

**63.03%** of respondents were categorized as improved.

The dashboard also showed an overall positive/recovery outcome pattern of approximately **99.35%** based on the outcome categories used in the dataset.

## Overall Interpretation

The dashboard shows that **delayed care-seeking was common in the dataset**, with differences observed across education, healthcare access, knowledge and perception, household decision-making, first actions, and healthcare pathways.

These findings are **descriptive associations within the dataset** and should not be interpreted as evidence of causation.
